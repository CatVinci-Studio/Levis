# Literature review

## Remote work and productivity

A growing body of research has looked at the question of whether working from home makes people more or less productive, and the findings that have been reported so far are, to put it mildly, quite mixed. Bloom et al. (2015) found in a randomised trial at a Chinese travel agency that call-centre employees who worked from home were 13% more productive, partly because they took fewer breaks and sick days. On the other hand, Gibbs, Mengel and Siemroth (2023) found that IT professionals in India logged more hours but produced less output after the switch to remote work in 2020. It is basically clear that the effect depends a lot on the kind of task and on how easy it is to monitor output (Emanuel & Harrington, 2024).

## Method

We surveyed 412 employees at three firms. The results show that remote work causes higher job satisfaction and prove that hybrid schedules are the best arrangement for every team.
